Why November, not December

The postal services publish their December shipping-deadline calendars every year, and the cheap services close first. A gift ordered in early November usually arrives on a ground rate; the same order placed December 20 pays for the fast lane or misses the day. The tracker on this page ships with its mail-by date set to December 15, 2026 for exactly this reason.

Money behaves better in slices. An $800 gift budget spread across three months hurts less than the same number on one January statement. The slices only happen if the recipient list and the caps exist before the stores do their seasonal push.

Early lists can also change. Kids' wish lists tend to settle by late November, and a purchase made with weeks to spare leaves room for a return or a size swap. A purchase made on the 23rd leaves whatever the shelf has.

Five quick tips

Write the whole list before buying anything
The list is where impulse spending gets caught, on paper where it is visible and comparable.
Set one total cap and small caps per recipient
A number per person beats a vague intention to spend less, and the tracker's Budget Summary holds you to it.
Track cards separately from gifts
Cards and parcels have different last-mail dates. The tracker keeps a 40-row address book with its own sent flags for this.
Keep an ideas log all year
A note on your phone in March is cheaper than a good idea remembered too late in December. The Ideas Log sheet gives the note a rating column.
Print the paper stuff early
Your printer, the office printer, and the print shop all get busier closer to the holidays, and toner always runs out at the worst row.
