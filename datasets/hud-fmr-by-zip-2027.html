FY2027 fair market rent for 51,871 ZIP rows across 38,601 ZIP codes, the way HUD publishes it: one row per ZIP per HUD area, ten columns with the area code, the metro or nonmetro flag, the area name, the state code, and dollar rents for studios through 4 bedrooms. Rates are effective October 1, 2026. The 27,288 metro rows and 24,583 nonmetro rows together span 2,600 HUD area codes (649 metro, 1,951 nonmetro) and 52 states and territories.

The rent split worth knowing: with 2BR, the 25th percentile sits at 1,020 dollars, the median at 1,190 and the 75th at 1,590. The cheapest row is ZIP 00669 in Aguadilla, PR at 520 and the most expensive is ZIP 95060 in Santa Cruz-Watsonville, CA at 5,260. Texas carries more rows than any other state at 3,244, and the ten biggest states (Texas, California, Pennsylvania, New York, Illinois, Missouri, Ohio, Iowa, Michigan, Florida) together hold 21,379 of the 51,871 rows. State-level 2BR medians run from California at 2,600 down to Puerto Rico at 610.

Integrity checks run at build time, all passing: zero malformed ZIP fields, zero empty or zero-valued rent cells, zero duplicate (zip, hud_area_code) keys, and zero rows where the 4BR rent is below the 2BR. The copy served here is byte-identical to the parsed source, confirmed by sha256, and 3,000 ZIP codes starting with 0 keep their leading zero. Full details live in schema.md.

Source: US Department of Housing and Urban Development, FY2027 Fair Market Rent release (FY27_FMRs.xlsx plus the FY2027 small-area FMR file), effective October 1, 2026. US government data, public domain.

FMR by ZIP: questions

Is the full FY2027 ZIP-level file free to download?
Yes. The CSV downloads straight from this page with no signup, and no cut-down version exists here: all 51,871 rows ship, zero empty cells and zero duplicate keys included. HUD publishes federal fair market rents as US government data, so the file is public domain and you can republish it or sell derived work without asking anyone.
Why does one ZIP code show up more than once in the file?
HUD's design, not a bug. 10,093 of the 38,601 ZIPs carry more than one row because metro and nonmetro coverage split the same ZIP between areas, and small-area county groups can add their own rows. ZIP 76437 in Texas, for example, holds a metro row for Abilene plus nonmetro rows for Eastland and Stephens counties, six rows total at most. Deduplicate on the zip and hud_area_code pair, or filter the metro flag, before per-ZIP lookups.
How do I query the file with SQL?
The file loads straight into DuckDB or SQLite. Texas carries the most rows at 3,244, the national 2BR median across all rows is 1,190 dollars, and a JOIN to the metro medians table attaches area rollups to ZIP rows. The SQL guide walks the percentile window function, the state ranking GROUP BY and the metro JOIN with copy-paste queries against this exact CSV.
