HUD FMR FY2026 by County: Completeness & Spread

3,229 counties56 states & territories0 empty cells8 range countiesfree full CSV

Every county-level row from HUD's FY2026 Fair Market Rent release: 3,229 county-equivalents across 56 states and territories, each with median and max FMR for studios through 4-bedroom units, plus metro status and an area_rows count of how many FMR areas the county covers in HUD's raw file. Zero empty cells anywhere. California contributes 58 counties, Texas 254, and Puerto Rico, Guam, the Virgin Islands, American Samoa and the Northern Marianas are all in the file.

The completeness check is the useful part. In 3,221 of the 3,229 counties, the 2BR max equals the 2BR median exactly, because HUD publishes a single rent per bedroom size whenever a county sits in one FMR area. Only 8 counties carry a real range, and all 8 are metro counties in Maine, New Hampshire, Massachusetts and Rhode Island, where HUD splits the county into multiple FMR areas. The widest is Bristol County, MA: 2BR median $1,729, max $2,550, a 47.5% gap. Neighboring Plymouth County runs $2,311 to $2,941.

What the file shows elsewhere: the national county median for a 2BR is $1,018. Santa Cruz County, CA tops the table at $4,214, and the cheapest 2BRs are Puerto Rico municipios at $475. The typical county's 4BR median runs 1.95 times its studio median, with the ladder flattest at 1.52x and steepest at 2.43x.

The medians and maxes are HUD's own county aggregates, straight from the release file; the gaps, the completeness counts and the ladder ratios are computed from those rows. When you compare a county here with a ZIP-level rent in the FY2026 ZIP dataset, expect small differences: ZIP rents roll up to the county median, they don't reproduce it.

Use it to screen county rent ceilings for voucher work, spot multi-FMR-area counties before you join ZIP data, or sanity-check any county against the 3,229-row distribution. For the ZIP-level version with 51,895 rows, see the HUD FMR FY2026 dataset; the HUD FMR by county guide walks the county rollup column by column, and small-area FMRs explains when HUD rents by ZIP instead. The full data dictionary lives in schema.md.

Source: US Department of Housing and Urban Development, FY2026 Fair Market Rent release (FY26_FMRs.xlsx on huduser.gov). US government data, public domain.
