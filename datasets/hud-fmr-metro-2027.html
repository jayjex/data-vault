HUD Fair Market Rents FY2027, Top-50 Metro Areas

50 metros × 2 tables10,143 ZIPs covered5 bedroom sizesfree full data

FY2027 fair market rents for the 50 metro FMR areas with the most ZIP codes, one row per area: the HUD area code and name, ZIP count, and a median across the area's ZIPs for each bedroom size, studios through 4BR. Rates are effective October 1, 2026. HUD publishes rents per ZIP; this table rolls them up to the metro, which is the level most rent comparisons actually happen at. Together the 50 areas cover 10,143 of the 27,288 metro-assigned ZIPs in the FY2027 file.

Two cuts, two different leaders. Rank by ZIP coverage and Washington DC leads with 488 ZIPs at a $2,400 2BR median, ahead of Los Angeles (474, $2,960) and Philadelphia (473, $1,900). Rank all 649 metro areas by 2BR price and Santa Cruz-Watsonville tops the list at $4,250 with 23 ZIPs, ahead of San Francisco ($3,590) and New York ($3,295). Only 12 areas appear in both top-50s, which is why the pack ships both tables. The price table is its own angle: 16 of the 50 priciest metros are in California, New Jersey puts up 7, and the 50th spot, Nashua NH at $2,160, still beats every Texas metro in the coverage table.

The aggregate is a plain median: every ZIP in the area weighs the same. That makes it easy to recompute and easy to check against the ZIP file, but it is not HUD's official 40th-percentile area FMR, and it is not a ZIP-count-weighted average. Where you want the official number, HUD publishes it at huduser.gov.

Everything here is recomputed from the ZIP-level FY2027 file behind the HUD FMR FY2027 dataset, not copied from any HUD summary, and the build asserts each metro code carries exactly one name and one state. The full column dictionary and caveats are in schema.md. For county-level FY2026 rates, see the HUD FMR by county dataset.

Source: US Department of Housing and Urban Development, FY2027 Fair Market Rent release, effective October 1, 2026. US government data, public domain.
