Occupation skills and wages map: 1,016 careers, their top 8 skills, and the US median wage

1,016 occupations19 columns956 with a wage867 SOC codessnapshot 2026-09-12

O*NET describes work. BLS prices occupations. The two files share no key, so answering "what does a job that needs negotiation pay" means a join. This table is that join: one row per detailed occupation in the O*NET 31.0 Database, carrying its eight highest-importance skill statements plus the median wage and employment figure BLS publishes for the SOC code underneath.

Built 2026-09-12 from two frozen sources: the O*NET 31.0 Database and the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics national table for May 2025. Both stay free at their own sites. What you are looking at is the join, the flags that make the join safe to aggregate, and a column-by-column account of where each field came from.

This is a staged build, not a live listing: no Getly product exists for it yet, so nothing on this page takes money. The 25 rows below are the free preview.

Fill rates, blanks, and the repeat flag

  • wages_median filled on 956 of 1,016 rows (94.1%); title on 961; job_zone on 923; all eight skills on 910; skill_count is 0 on 106 rows.
  • 60 rows have no wage. 19 are military occupations (codes starting 55-) because BLS does not publish wages for them, and the other 41 sit under 35 SOC codes with no national estimate. Five of those are occupations you would expect to be paid: Actors, Dancers and Choreographers, Musicians and Singers, Disc Jockeys Except Radio, and the Entertainers and Performers All Other residual.
  • 76 SOC codes carry more than one detailed occupation, so 225 rows repeat a wage that belongs to the parent code. Chief Executives (11-1011.00) and Chief Sustainability Officers (11-1011.03) both read 213,990, because BLS publishes one number for 11-1011.
  • That flag is why the employment column needs care: summing total_employment across every row gives 200,074,360; across one row per SOC code it gives 145,763,920. Filter wage_repeated = N when you want a row per published wage.
  • Lowest published wage in the file: 30,890 (Cooks, Fast Food). Highest: 559,030 (Pediatric Surgeons). Median 64,950; nearest-rank 10th percentile 39,850, 90th 125,470. Median hourly 30.47.

Two joins the file makes easy

A skill, ranked by pay. The companion long file puts one occupation-skill pair on each of its 7,280 rows, and the wage rides along, so a filter and a sort answer "what does work using this skill pay". Filter skill_name to Troubleshooting and 77 occupations come back; Programming returns 15; Active Listening appears in 859 occupations, the widest reach of the 33 skill statements in the file.

Same skills, different pay. Public Relations Managers and Writers and Authors share six of their eight skill statements: Active Listening, Complex Problem Solving, Critical Thinking, Reading Comprehension, Speaking, Writing. The wage gap between them is 70,000 dollars (146,910 against 76,910). Electrical Engineers and Bookkeeping, Accounting and Auditing Clerks share six of eight as well, and sit 69,960 apart. A skill list is not a pay grade, and this file lets you show that with two rows instead of an argument.

What is not here

  • National numbers only. No state, metro, industry or experience-level cuts.
  • Medians only. BLS publishes 10th and 90th percentile wages; this file carries neither, nor the standard errors.
  • No Importance, Level or Frequency scores, just the eight statements in our rank order. The long file carries skill_rank and skill_source on its 7,280 rows: 4,383 essential and 2,897 transferable pairings, with the wage and job zone repeated on each row.
  • soc_code comes from cutting the O*NET-SOC code at the decimal. O*NET publishes a crosswalk table for that mapping and it is not redistributed here, so the derivation is yours to check on 1,016 rows.
  • Wages are BLS estimates for occupations across the country, not salaries at any employer, and they move when BLS or O*NET ships a release.

Modifications to the source database, all of them listed in docs/CHANGES.md inside the pack:

  • Added soc_code, derived by cutting the O*NET-SOC code at the decimal (15-1252.00 becomes 15-1252). That is our rule, not the official O*NET-SOC to SOC crosswalk file, which is not in the pack.
  • Added title, wages_median, wages_median_hourly and total_employment from outside O*NET: they come from the BLS OEWS May 2025 national table, joined on soc_code at the detailed occupation level, so 23-1011.00 Lawyers carries the wage BLS reports for 23-1011.
  • Added skill_1 through skill_8, selected from the Importance ratings in the essential and transferable skill tables, sorted high to low, ties broken alphabetically, duplicate element names removed inside an occupation, first eight kept. That order is our ranking, not an O*NET ordering.
  • Dropped what we did not use: numeric ratings, standard errors, confidence bounds, scale metadata, element IDs, dates and domain source columns. No Importance, Level or Frequency value appears in either file.
  • Dropped skill rows flagged Recommend Suppress = Y.
  • Kept job_zone unchanged from the job zones table, first row per code.
  • Renamed every header to snake_case ASCII.
  • Left cells blank where a detailed match was missing instead of substituting group-level or state-level data. The 54 SOC codes involved are listed in docs/unmatched-soc-codes.txt.
  • Kept one row per O*NET-SOC occupation and made the wage repeats explicit rather than hiding them, with soc_row_count and wage_repeated as two derived columns carrying that fact.

Wage and employment columns come from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, national table, May 2025 release (bls.gov/oes), which is US government public-domain data; BLS asks that the source be cited, not that it be hidden. Job zone and O*NET-SOC codes come from O*NET, which is why the paragraph above governs reuse of this derived table.
