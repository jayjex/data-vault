OpenStreetMap Civic Amenities: New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, Austin, Seattle

4,000 elements, 5 cities3 amenity types2026-09-10 countedfree CSV

One CSV, fifteen rows: five big US cities × three civic amenity types. Each row is the total count of OpenStreetMap elements with that amenity tag inside the city's bounding box, queried from the Overpass API in one snapshot.

The amenity tags are amenity=drinking_water (public fountains and tap points), amenity=public_bookcase (street-side book-sharing shelves), and amenity=bicycle_repair_station (public stands with tools or an air pump). Queries match nodes, ways, and relations (nwr), since fountains are sometimes mapped as ways and repair stations as small way polygons.

The spread is the story. New York City has 1,599 mapped drinking water points, more than the other four cities combined. Seattle leads public bookcases at 497, nearly half of the five-city total of 1,004, which fits a city where Little Free Library-style shelves became a civic habit. Bike repair stations are rare everywhere, from 28 in Austin to 44 in Los Angeles, the sort of tag mapped only where a city or advocacy group installed stands deliberately.

Counts ran on 2026-09-10 via Overpass queries of the form nwr(south,west,north,east)["amenity"="drinking_water"]; out count;, using city bounding boxes rather than exact admin boundaries, so the numbers include small overhangs into neighboring municipalities and are a snapshot of a database that moves daily.

For the same three amenity types in Tokyo, Jakarta, and Seoul, see the OSM Asia civic amenities dataset — same query shape, so the two tables compare directly. For open civic-adjacent macro data on the European side, the Eurostat house price index dataset and the Eurostat actual rents dataset cover NL and EU-27 housing. The newest region in the series is Africa: the OSM Africa civic amenities dataset counts Lagos, Nairobi, and Addis Ababa. The OSM Oceania civic amenities dataset covers Sydney, Melbourne, and Auckland with a drinking-water-toilets-bench trio. A second US table, the OSM US New York-Chicago-Seattle civic amenities dataset, counts the same trio inside exact city limits.
