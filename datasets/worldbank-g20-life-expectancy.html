World Bank Life Expectancy, G20 Countries + EU27

1,300 rows20 entities65 yearsfree full data

Life expectancy at birth, in years, for the 19 national G20 members plus the European Union aggregate, from the World Bank's WDI indicator SP.DYN.LE00.IN. One row per entity-year, 1960 through 2024, four columns: entity name, ISO3 code, year, and life_expectancy_years. It completes a trio with the World Bank population pack and the rural population share pack, all three on the same 20 entities and the same entity-year key, so the files join without remapping.

Japan has held the top spot since 1972, 53 straight years, and stands at 84.04 in 2024. Canada led from 1960 through 1971 before that. Behind Japan sit Italy at 83.95, Korea at 83.63, and Australia at 83.05. South Africa is lowest at 66.31, and that is a record high for the country, so the top-to-bottom gap of 17.7 years overstates how the members are trending apart.

The biggest mover since 1960 is China, up 44.6 years from 33.42 to 78.02. The 33.42 starting value in 1960 is the lowest number anywhere in the file. Saudi Arabia added 33.67 years off a 45.31 start, Korea 29.83 off 53.80. The slowest gains are Russia's: up 5.91 years over the whole window, with a trough of 64.47 in 1994 that the country did not climb back past until 2007 relative to its own 1960 level. The United States gained 9.12 years, and its path runs 78.79 in 2019, down to 76.33 in 2021, then back above the 2019 mark in 2024 at 78.89.

Fourteen of the 20 entities set record highs in 2024. The other six peaked earlier: Germany, Canada, and Turkiye in 2019, Japan in 2020, Australia in 2021, and China in 2022, which makes pre-2020 baselines the right comparison for those six. The world population milestones guide reads the sibling population file, and the rural population share guide covers the third pack in the set.

The source is one keyless call to the World Bank API v2, and the pack keeps the Bank's entity names verbatim ("Korea, Rep.", "Turkiye", "Russian Federation"). Values are rounded to two decimals; the WDI publishes this series as annual estimates, so the rounding discards nothing a model could use. The series ends at 2024 because WDI has not published 2025 life expectancy yet, a longer lag than the population counts. The full column dictionary and caveats are in schema.md.

Source: World Bank World Development Indicators via api.worldbank.org, indicator SP.DYN.LE00.IN, retrieved 2026-09-10, WDI last updated 2026-07-13. License CC BY 4.0.
