World Bank Rural Population Share, G20 Countries + EU27

1,320 rows20 entities66 yearsfree full data

The rural share of total population for the 19 national G20 members plus the European Union aggregate, from the World Bank's WDI indicator SP.RUR.TOTL.ZS. One row per entity-year, 1960 through 2025, four columns: entity name, ISO3 code, year, and rural_pct. It pairs with the World Bank population pack on the same entity list: multiply rural_pct by that pack's population to get rural headcounts.

India is the one G20 member still majority rural in 2025, at 64.31%, about 941 million people against its 1.46 billion total. Its share has drifted down slowly, 69.1% in 2010 to 64.31% now, under half a point a year, so the file ends before its rural majority does.

China crossed below 50% rural in 2011 and has since fallen to 33.66%, roughly 473 million people still rural in absolute terms. The widest movers since 1960: Turkiye dropped 66.1 points (76.57 to 10.51), Korea 53.5, Saudi Arabia 53.2, China 46.6, Indonesia 44.8. Relative to their 1960 shares, Japan and Brazil both shed about 79% of their rural percentage. The smallest moves are Australia (6.1 points), Germany (6.4), and the United Kingdom (4.8).

Two quirks worth knowing before you model this. Korea's rural share bottomed at 18.03% in 2010 and has crept back up to 18.83%, one of the few reversals in the set, and the United States is the other one, off a 19.30% bottom the same year; the rural population share by country guide reads the reversals, the crossover years, and the 66-year drops entity by entity. And peak rural share is 1960 for 18 of 20 entities; China peaked in 1963 and the UK in 1971, so treating 1960 as the baseline is safe for all but those two.

For the demographic outcome behind those shares, the life expectancy pack runs the WDI's SP.DYN.LE00.IN series on the same 20 entities: Japan has led since 1972 at 84.04 in 2024, and China's 44.6-year gain since 1960 is the largest in the set.

The source is one keyless call to the World Bank API v2, and the pack keeps the API's values verbatim: no rounding, no imputation, entity names as the Bank spells them ("Korea, Rep.", "Turkiye", "Russian Federation"). The Bank's "rural" follows each country's own census definition, so the threshold is not harmonized across countries. The full column dictionary and caveats are in schema.md.

Source: World Bank World Development Indicators via api.worldbank.org, indicator SP.RUR.TOTL.ZS, retrieved 2026-09-10, WDI last updated 2026-07-13. License CC BY 4.0.
