Airbnb data by country: what exists and where it comes from

Country-level view around the Airbnb Listings, 6 US Cities pack · source counts from Inside Airbnb, September 2026 · verified links only.

There is no official Airbnb dataset by country. Airbnb publishes nothing at the country level, and the company's terms of service bar scraping, so every country-level picture is stitched together from three kinds of sources: city files scraped and republished by Inside Airbnb, tourism nights counted by Eurostat for EU countries, and national statistics offices for the rest. Coverage follows those sources, and the sources follow the law. The United States has 34 Inside Airbnb regions because US case law tolerates scraping public pages; much of Asia and Africa has none because no volunteer has taken on the work.

This catalog ships a 6-city US pack, so its own coverage stops at six cities. The map above points to the other countries; the granularity example below shows what good data looks like once you get there.

The three grains of "by country"

Listing-level rows. Inside Airbnb publishes listings.csv-style files per city, one snapshot per release. A "country" dataset built from these is a folder of city files with a country column you add yourself. This is the only grain that carries room types, minimum nights, and availability.

Aggregated city counts. Press coverage, academic papers, and commercial trackers roll city snapshots up to country totals. Fine for a headline, useless for analysis, because snapshot dates rarely match across cities.

National tourism statistics. Eurostat's tour_occ_ninat dataset counts nights spent at tourist accommodation establishments per country, residents and non-residents, monthly. Official, comparable, free, and not Airbnb-specific: hotels and campsites are in the same total. National offices such as INE (Spain), ISTAT (Italy), INSEE (France), and Destatis (Germany) run the same kind of series domestically.

Why data quality differs by country

Regulation changes what is listed

A scraped dataset measures the platform after local law has filtered it. New York City's Local Law 18 removed most short-term rentals from Airbnb's platform starting September 2023, and our NYC snapshot reflects the residue: 30,234 rows where 42% are private rooms, a mix unlike any other US market in the pack. Amsterdam caps whole-home rentals at 30 nights a year and requires registration numbers, which shows up as thinner supply in the Amsterdam file. Where short stays are lightly regulated, such as many US markets, the same scraping method returns far denser data. Comparing countries without reading their rules compares rulebooks first.

Scraping legality runs on different tracks

In the United States, the Ninth Circuit's hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn decision (2022) left scraping publicly viewable pages on solid ground for now, and combined with no national privacy statute for public business data, Inside Airbnb's 34 US regions keep updating. In the EU, the same act gets weighed against GDPR and unfair-competition law, and individual host data is the sensitive part. Some countries with big Airbnb markets, Turkey, Thailand, Kenya among the 37, are covered by exactly one city region, which says more about volunteer effort and legal comfort than about rental volume.

GDPR shapes what European data can contain

GDPR treats host names, photos, and contact details as personal data, so republishing them for EU hosts carries real exposure. That is why public sources for European cities ship anonymized summary fields, and why Airbnb pushed Inside Airbnb to drop some city releases over the years. Our own pack carries zero host identity columns even for US rows: id, neighbourhood as a ZIP area, room type, nightly price, and availability are what a safe public dataset contains. The Airbnb data guide walks the full 12-column schema.

FAQ

Is there Airbnb data for every country?

No. Airbnb publishes no official country-level dataset. The closest thing is Inside Airbnb, which in September 2026 listed 123 city-level regions across 37 countries, with 34 of those regions in the United States alone. Much of Asia, Africa, and Eastern Europe has no regularly updated coverage at all. For EU countries, Eurostat tourism statistics are the reliable fallback, though they count all tourist accommodation, not Airbnb listings.

What is the best free source for Airbnb data by country?

Inside Airbnb's get-the-data page for listing-level city files (CC BY 4.0), and the Eurostat databrowser for EU-wide nights spent in tourist accommodation per country. Both are free. National statistics offices such as INE (Spain), ISTAT (Italy), INSEE (France), and Destatis (Germany) publish tourism accommodation series that go back further than any scraping project, but without Airbnb-specific detail.

Why does Airbnb data quality differ so much between countries?

Three reasons. Regulation: registration schemes and short-stay bans change what is even listed on the platform, so a country's listing count measures its rules as much as its market. Scraping legality: US courts have treated public-page scraping as mostly permissible since hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn (2022), while EU jurisdictions weigh the same act against GDPR, so private scraping projects thin out in Europe. Privacy law: GDPR treats host names and contact details as personal data, which is why public sources for European cities ship anonymized summaries only.
