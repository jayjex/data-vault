Airbnb price data exploration: inside the Austin listings file

Published 2026-09-10. Source: Inside Airbnb, CC BY 4.0. Data Vault is not affiliated with Airbnb or Inside Airbnb. This page documents the data; it is not pricing or investment advice.

Most "airbnb price data" pages hand you one blended national number and move on. This one works the other way: we took the Austin file from our 90,169-row 6-city pack, 11,295 listings captured 2026-06-22, and computed everything a first exploration should produce. Median price per room type, the busiest ZIP codes, the price distribution from p25 to p95, how availability_365 splits, and one quirk we did not expect: listings whose minimum_nights crosses 1,000. Every figure below was computed from the shipped CSV with Python, and the last section shows the code so you can rerun it.

The file we explored

Austin is one of six city files in the Airbnb Listings, 6 US Cities pack. Its summary file carries 11,295 rows and 12 columns, snapshot 2026-06-22, neighborhoods given as 43 distinct ZIP codes. The raw snapshot comes from Inside Airbnb's get-the-data page, published under CC BY 4.0; our pack normalizes the six cities into one schema. Two quirks to know before you compute anything: 974 rows have an empty price, and one row has an empty minimum_nights. The stats below exclude blanks and say so where it matters.

Of the 11,295 rows, 9,354 are entire homes or apartments, 1,665 private rooms, 201 hotel rooms, and 75 shared rooms. Medians below use priced rows only:

The shape tells you more than any single number. Half the market sits between $133.10 and $363.00, a $230 band. Then the tail takes over: from p95 to max, the price multiplies by 60, with the most expensive Austin listing at $58,600.50 per night. That tail is why the median, not the mean, is the honest answer to "what does an Austin listing cost", and why per-room-type medians in the section above stay stable while means would swing with a handful of luxury rows.

The outlier: minimum_nights over 1,000

Austin's own numbers are tame: median minimum_nights is 2, 4,640 listings allow a single night, and the maximum is 365. The pack-wide view is where the oddity lives. Five listings in the New York City file set a minimum stay of 1,000 nights or more:

A 1,124-night minimum is 3.1 years. No vacation rental works that way, and all five rows have an empty price, so they contribute nothing to nightly-rate stats anyway. What they look like is long-term housing postings riding the short-term-rental schema, plausibly a workaround in a city with strict rules on short stays. Five rows out of 90,169 would not move a mean, but they change how you read the minimum_nights column: high values there mark a different kind of listing, not a demanding host. Filter or segment on minimum_nights >= 365 before you compute anything nightly.

Reproduce the numbers

The whole exploration is a dozen lines of standard library. Against the pack's Austin file:

import csv, statistics
rows = list(csv.DictReader(open("cities/austin/listings-summary.csv")))
prices = sorted(float(r["price"]) for r in rows if r["price"])

def pct(p):
    k = (len(prices) - 1) * p / 100
    f = int(k)
    return prices[f] + (prices[min(f + 1, len(prices) - 1)] - prices[f]) * (k - f)

print(statistics.median(prices), pct(25), pct(50), pct(75), pct(95))
# 217.0 133.1 217.0 363.0 969.0

FAQ

What is the median Airbnb price in the Austin dataset?

$217 per night across the 10,321 priced listings, snapshot 2026-06-22. By room type: entire home/apt $233.38 (8,548 priced rows), private room $95.72 (1,516), hotel room $314.00 (186), shared room $16.81 (71). The middle half of priced listings falls between $133.10 and $363.00.

Which Austin neighborhoods have the most Airbnb listings?

ZIP code 78704 leads with 1,684 listings, followed by 78702 (1,210), 78701 (1,147), 78745 (645), and 78741 (541). Those five ZIPs hold 5,227 of the 11,295 rows, 46.3% of the file, out of 43 ZIP codes total.

What does a minimum_nights value of 1,000 or more mean?

In the 90,169-row pack, 5 listings set a minimum stay between 1,000 and 1,124 nights, all in the New York City file. A 1,124-night minimum is more than three years, so nobody books those rooms as nightly rentals; the rows behave as long-term housing postings riding the listings schema. Austin's highest minimum_nights is 365. Segment or drop these rows before computing nightly-market stats.
