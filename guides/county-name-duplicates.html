County name duplicates: what rent does when states share a county name

Published 2026-09-10. Data source: the FY2027 fair market rent file by county, 3,229 rows, md5 3960f577f991978f3a1bb99d353853d5, every number on this page computed from that file on 2026-09-10. Two-bedroom county medians throughout, dollars per month, no product prices involved. This page documents the data.

423 county names shared across state lines, 1,686 counties3,213 widest same-name rent gap, Santa Cruz AZ to CA

The short answer

A county name is not an address. In the FY2027 county file, 1,966 distinct names cover 3,229 county rows, and 423 of those names are claimed by two or more states, 1,686 counties in total, 52.2 percent of the file. Washington County is the most shared name in America: 30 states each have one, from Washington County, Kansas, to Washington County, Rhode Island. Jefferson County follows in 25 states, Franklin in 24, Jackson and Lincoln in 23 each, Madison in 19. Thirty-six names reach ten or more states; 223 more live in exactly two.

Rent separates the copies. Washington County medians 1,187 across its 30 counties, but the floors and ceilings tell the story: 905 in Mississippi, 1,879 in Rhode Island. Jefferson County stretches 905 in Mississippi to 2,169 in Colorado, Franklin County 905 to 2,165 in Vermont, and Montgomery County tops the board below with a 2,438 ceiling in Maryland against a 905 floor in Alabama. The widest gap inside any single name belongs to Santa Cruz County: the Arizona county medians 1,041, the California county medians 4,254, a 3,213 span. Orange County runs 1,014 in Indiana to 3,057 in California, a 2,043 gap, and Putnam County 942 in Illinois to 2,971 in New York. Forty-six of the 423 duplicated names carry a ceiling at or above 2,000.

The cheap ends pile up in the same few states. Mississippi anchors 30 of the 423 floors at 905, its cheapest county median, and five South Carolina groups share the lowest group floor of all at 885, Marion County among them. The middle of the distribution is quiet: the 423 group medians median 1,071, near the file-wide county median of 1,063, and the drama lives in the tails. Every board row and the full state lists behind it come from the one-row-per-name CSV, and the SQL block at the end of this page returns the table verbatim.

Methodology: the file and what each number is

The source is the FY2027 fair market rent file by county: 3,229 county-equivalent rows across 56 states and territories, parsed from HUD's FY27 workbooks and shipped beside the FY2026 county dataset (same 15-column schema, md5 of the FY2027 file 3960f577f991978f3a1bb99d353853d5). Every number on this page uses the fmr_2br_median column. A name group is an exact match on the county column, suffix included, so Louisiana's Washington Parish, Puerto Rico's Municipio rows and the independent-city rows never mix into a County group, and all 423 groups here are pure County names. No state carries two counties of the same name, the file's 3,229 state and county pairs are distinct, so each group's county count equals its state count.

The group median is the median of the member counties' fmr_2br_median values, the middle row for odd counts, the mean of the two middle rows rounded up for even counts, which is how the CSV and the SQL below both compute it. Floor and ceiling name the member county with the lowest and highest median, ties resolved by state code in alphabetical order, and the board orders by states descending, then county name. Every number was computed two ways over the source file, a python pass and an independent SQLite pass, and the page SQL re-executed verbatim after publication, with zero mismatches across all 423 rows.

Recompute the board yourself

Load the source CSV into SQLite as county27, fifteen columns per row. One block returns the whole board:

-- SQLite: the ten most shared county names by state count, with 2BR extremes
CREATE TABLE county27 (state TEXT, state_name TEXT, county TEXT, metro TEXT,
  fmr_0br_median INT, fmr_0br_max INT, fmr_1br_median INT, fmr_1br_max INT,
  fmr_2br_median INT, fmr_2br_max INT, fmr_3br_median INT, fmr_3br_max INT,
  fmr_4br_median INT, fmr_4br_max INT, area_rows INT);
.import --skip 1 fmr-by-county-2027.csv county27

WITH g AS (SELECT county, COUNT(DISTINCT state) AS sc, COUNT(*) AS cr
           FROM county27 GROUP BY county HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT state) > 1),
med AS (SELECT c.county, ROUND(AVG(c.fmr_2br_median)) AS med
        FROM (SELECT county, fmr_2br_median,
                     ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY county ORDER BY fmr_2br_median, state) AS r2,
                     COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY county) AS n
              FROM county27) c
        WHERE c.county IN (SELECT county FROM g)
          AND c.r2 IN ((c.n+1)/2, (c.n+2)/2) GROUP BY c.county),
lo AS (SELECT county, MIN(fmr_2br_median) AS mn FROM county27
       WHERE county IN (SELECT county FROM g) GROUP BY county),
hi AS (SELECT county, MAX(fmr_2br_median) AS mx FROM county27
       WHERE county IN (SELECT county FROM g) GROUP BY county),
ls AS (SELECT county, state, fmr_2br_median,
              ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY county ORDER BY fmr_2br_median, state) AS rl
       FROM county27 WHERE county IN (SELECT county FROM g)),
hs AS (SELECT county, state, fmr_2br_median,
              ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY county ORDER BY fmr_2br_median DESC, state) AS rh
       FROM county27 WHERE county IN (SELECT county FROM g)),
srun AS (SELECT county, state,
                GROUP_CONCAT(state, ' ') OVER (PARTITION BY county ORDER BY state) AS acc,
                ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY county ORDER BY state) AS rs,
                COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY county) AS ns
         FROM (SELECT DISTINCT county, state FROM county27)),
states AS (SELECT county, acc AS states FROM srun WHERE rs = ns)
SELECT g.county, g.sc, states.states, g.cr, med.med, lo.mn, ls.state, hi.mx, hs.state
FROM g JOIN med ON med.county=g.county JOIN lo ON lo.county=g.county JOIN hi ON hi.county=g.county
JOIN ls ON ls.county=g.county AND ls.fmr_2br_median=lo.mn AND ls.rl=1
JOIN hs ON hs.county=g.county AND hs.fmr_2br_median=hi.mx AND hs.rh=1
JOIN states ON states.county=g.county
ORDER BY g.sc DESC, g.county
LIMIT 10;

Run in one session, the block returns exactly the ten rows quoted in the comments, including the tie order Jackson before Lincoln and Clay before Montgomery. Ordering by MAX(fmr_2br_median) - MIN(fmr_2br_median) DESC instead turns the board into a same-name spread ranking with Santa Cruz County on top, or drop the HAVING clause to see the 1,543 single-state names this page leaves out.

County name duplicates: questions

How many county names repeat across state lines?
In the FY2027 fair market rent file by county, 423 county names are shared by two or more states, covering 1,686 of the 3,229 county rows, 52.2 percent of the file. 223 of those names live in exactly two states and 36 appear in ten or more, led by Washington County at 30, Jefferson County at 25, Franklin County at 24, then Jackson and Lincoln counties at 23 each.
Which duplicated county name has the widest rent gap?
Santa Cruz County: the Arizona county medians 1,041 for a two-bedroom and the California county medians 4,254, a 3,213 gap inside one name. Orange County runs second at 2,043 between Indiana at 1,014 and California at 3,057, Putnam County third at 2,029 between Illinois and New York, and 46 of the 423 duplicated names have a ceiling at or above 2,000.
Why does the same county name cost so little in some states?
The cheap end of most duplicated names sits in the same places. Mississippi anchors 30 of the 423 floors at 905, the cheapest county median the state posts, and five South Carolina groups tie for the lowest group floor of all at 885, Marion County among them. Those are county-level rents in low-cost states, not the national floor: the lowest 2BR county median in the file is 517, in Puerto Rico, whose rows use Municipio names and never enter a County-name group.
