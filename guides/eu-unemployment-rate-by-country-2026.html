EU unemployment rate by country in 2026, month by month

Eurostat table une_rt_m, seasonally adjusted, unit PC_ACT (percent of the labour force), age band TOTAL (16-74), retrieved 2026-09-10 (source dataset updated 2026-09-04), published 2026-09-10. Data source: Eurostat, CC BY 4.0. Data Vault is not affiliated with Eurostat. This page documents the data; it is not economic or policy advice.

What this page measures

Every number here comes from the free CSV behind Eurostat Monthly Unemployment Rate by Sex: 7,641 rows covering the EU-27 aggregate plus eight countries (Germany, Greece, Spain, France, Italy, Netherlands, Poland, Sweden), one row per geo-month-sex from January 2003 through July 2026. The grid is complete, 9 geos × 283 months × 3 sexes with no gaps, and the rates are seasonally adjusted, so month-to-month moves are not calendar artefacts.

Two conventions run through the page. First, the headline table uses the total (both sexes) rates; the male and female splits get their own section. Second, this file covers nine geos, not all 27 EU members, so "highest" and "lowest" below mean highest and lowest inside that slice.

July 2026, all nine geos

The EU-27 rate reads 6.1 percent in July 2026. It has sat at that level since February, after 6.0 in January, and every month since the start of 2025 has landed inside a 5.9 to 6.1 band. Poland anchors the low end at 3.4 percent; Spain is the last of the nine above 10. Across the set, the spread runs 2.9× from the lowest to the highest rate.

The crisis peaks behind the 2026 numbers

Seven of the nine series peak in 2013 or 2014, the euro-area crisis years, and the file shows the shape of that crisis month by month rather than year by year:

  • Greece holds the file's record at 28.3 percent, July 2013. Seven years earlier, in May 2008, the same series read 7.3, its lowest point. For Greek women the record is higher still: 32.3 percent in September 2013, the single highest value anywhere in the 7,641 rows.
  • Spain spent 70 consecutive months above 20 percent (August 2010 through May 2016) around its 26.4 percent peak in February 2013. Counting the two earlier 2010 months, 72 of the file's 283 months sit at or above 20 for Spain, and March 2008 is the last month its rate was below 10 percent.
  • The EU-27 aggregate peaked at 11.7 percent and stayed there for five straight months, January through May 2013, against 9.8 when the series opens in January 2003 and 7.1 at its pre-crisis low in February 2008.
  • Poland's peak is the oldest, 20.0 percent in August 2003, seven months into the series, and the file starts already deep in it: 19.9 in January 2003.
  • Sweden is the outlier. Its series high of 9.8 percent is recent, January 2025, and its 8.6 in July 2026 is the only one of the nine rates still above where the series stood in July 2003 (6.6).

Males and females in July 2026

The file carries each rate split by sex, and in July 2026 the female rate runs above the male rate in six of the nine geos. The gap is widest in Greece at 4.5 points, then Spain at 2.3 and Italy at 1.4. Germany runs the other way at −0.6, the only geo where the male rate exceeds the female rate by half a point or more.

Read the series yourself

The full 7,641-row file downloads free on the monthly dataset page under CC BY 4.0, alongside a 22-row sample (sample.csv, sample.json) and a schema. Seven columns per row: month, geo, sex, age, value, plus Eurostat's labels for geo, sex and age. Filter to sex=T for the headline rates above, or geo=EU27 for the aggregate, and every table on this page reproduces in minutes. If you republish derived numbers, the dataset license guide covers what CC BY 4.0 attribution asks of you.

For the long run with age bands, the annual unemployment dataset covers the same countries through 2025 with seven age bands including youth, and the labour market data guide walks the annual file from the 2013 youth extremes to the 2025 headline rates.

EU unemployment 2026 questions

What is the EU unemployment rate in 2026?
The EU-27 seasonally adjusted unemployment rate stood at 6.1 percent in July 2026, where it has held since February 2026 after opening the year at 6.0 in January. Every month of 2025 and 2026 so far sits inside a 5.9 to 6.1 band. Across the nine geos in the Eurostat monthly file, July 2026 runs from Poland at 3.4 percent to Spain at 10.0 percent.
Which EU country has the highest unemployment rate in 2026?
Among the nine geos in the file, the EU-27 aggregate plus eight countries, Spain is highest at 10.0 percent in July 2026, followed by Sweden at 8.6 percent and Greece at 7.9 percent. Poland is lowest at 3.4 percent, with Germany and the Netherlands at 4.0. The file covers eight of the EU's 27 members, so it ranks the countries the catalog tracks rather than all of them.
When did EU unemployment peak?
The EU-27 aggregate peaked at 11.7 percent, a level it held for five straight months from January through May 2013. Spain peaked at 26.4 percent in February 2013 and then spent 70 consecutive months above 20 percent, and Greece set the file's all-time record at 28.3 percent in July 2013, 32.3 percent for women that September. Poland's 20.0 percent peak is the oldest in the file, from August 2003, seven months into the series.
