Dutch house prices vs rents, in Eurostat's own index data

Eurostat tables prc_hpi_a (house price index, annual) and prc_hicp_midx / prc_hicp_manr (HICP actual rentals, coicop CP0411), Netherlands and EU-27, retrieved 2026-09-10 (house prices last updated 2026-07-02, rents 2026-02-06), published 2026-09-10. Data source: Eurostat, CC BY 4.0. Data Vault is not affiliated with Eurostat. This page documents the data; it is not economic or investment advice.

What this page measures

Two free CSVs sit behind every number here. The Eurostat house price index dataset carries 342 rows of annual data for the Netherlands and the EU-27 aggregate, 2005 through 2025, split into total, newly built and existing dwellings. The HICP actual rents dataset carries 580 rows across two monthly tables: the rent index (2015=100, Netherlands from 2009-12, EU-27 from 2016-12) and the year-on-year rate of change.

Both indexes use 2015=100, so the two geos are directly comparable and the rent-versus-price gap is a ratio of the same base. One convention: "house prices" below means the total-dwelling annual average index (unit I15_A_AVG, purchase TOTAL) unless a sentence says otherwise.

The two decades in anchor years

Dutch prices closed 2025 at 215.15 on the 2015=100 base: 115.1 percent above the 2015 average and 102.4 percent above 2005. The EU-27 aggregate closed at 161.93, up 61.9 percent in the decade and 88.6 percent since 2005. The table below picks out the years that define the shape of both series:

The crash and the recovery ran on different clocks. The EU-27 aggregate needed eight years to pass its 2008 peak again (103.67 in 2008, 104.31 in 2016). The Netherlands needed ten: its 2008 level of 118.74 was not cleared until 2018's 124.4, because Dutch prices kept falling through 2013 while the EU aggregate had already stabilised.

Two years later the Dutch market has more than made up for it: 2024 and 2025 averaged above eight percent, so the series ended 2025 at 215.15, 17.4 percent above its 2023 level.

Rents never fall

The rent side of the file tells a different story. Across every month the rent datasets cover, from 2010-12 for the Netherlands and 2017-12 for the EU-27, the year-on-year rate has never once gone negative. The Dutch series bottomed at +0.8 percent in July 2021 and peaked at +5.4 percent in July 2024; the EU-27 series ran between +1.1 (June 2018) and +3.3 percent (July 2024). December 2025 reads +4.9 percent for the Netherlands and +3.2 percent for the EU-27.

Matched over the same window, Dutch sale prices grew 104.3 percent while Dutch rents grew 28.4 percent, and the EU-27 gap is 55.2 against 20.4. Rents are the steadier series, not the faster one: they missed the 2021-22 price spike almost entirely (Dutch rent inflation was 0.8 percent at the end of 2021) and they also skipped the 2023 correction, accelerating instead into 2024-25.

New and existing dwellings

The house price file splits each geo into newly built and existing dwellings, and the decade ordered them differently in each geography. In the EU-27, newly built dwellings led: +70.7 percent since 2015 against +59.7 percent for existing ones (170.66 vs 159.65 in 2025). In the Netherlands the order flips: existing dwellings did +115.1 percent against +110.9 for new builds, and 2025 closes with existing homes (215.10) indexed slightly above new ones (210.87). The Dutch split only starts in 2005 with both dwelling types near 104-106, so the flip is not a base-year artefact.

Read the series yourself

Both datasets download free under CC BY 4.0, each with a 22-row sample (sample.csv, sample.json). The house price file has eight columns: year, geo, purchase, unit, value, plus Eurostat's labels. The rent files share six columns with month in place of year. Filter to geo=NL, unit=I15_A_AVG, purchase=TOTAL and every house price number in the tables above reproduces in one pass. If you republish derived numbers, the dataset license guide covers what CC BY 4.0 attribution asks of you.

Dutch house price questions

How much have Dutch house prices risen?
Eurostat's house price index for the Netherlands closed 2025 at 215.15 on the 2015=100 base, which is 115.1 percent above the 2015 average and 102.4 percent above the 2005 level of 106.30. The final year-over-year print was +8.5 percent for 2025, after +8.2 percent in 2024.
Have Dutch or EU house prices ever fallen?
Yes, twice in the file. After the financial crisis the Netherlands posted five straight down years, from −4.4 percent in 2009 to −6.0 percent in 2013, and the EU-27 aggregate fell in 2009 (−3.7), 2012 (−2.1) and 2013 (−1.7). The only down year of the 2020s so far is 2023: −1.9 percent for the Netherlands and −0.3 percent for the EU-27, the first declines in both series since 2013.
Are European rents rising faster than house prices?
Over the last decade house prices grew far faster. From the 2016 average to the 2025 average the Dutch price index rose 104.3 percent against 28.4 percent for Dutch rents measured December 2016 to December 2025; the EU-27 figures are 55.2 percent for prices against 20.4 percent for rents. Rents are steadier, though: neither series has ever printed a negative month. Dutch rent inflation ran between 0.8 and 5.4 percent year on year, EU-27 between 1.1 and 3.3 percent.
