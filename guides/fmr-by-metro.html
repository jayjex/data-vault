HUD FMR by metro: the priciest and cheapest metro areas, FY2026

FY2026 edition, published 2026-09-10. Data source: HUD, public domain. Data Vault is not affiliated with HUD. This page documents the data; it is not housing or legal advice.

The short answer

HUD prices every metro FMR area separately, and the FY2026 file rewards the comparison: across the 649 metro areas in the HUD FMR FY2026 pack, the 2BR median runs from 520 dollars a month in Yauco, PR to 4,350 dollars in Santa Cruz-Watsonville, CA. That is an 8.4x spread between the most and least expensive metro in the country. The median across all 649 metro medians sits at 1,210, a touch above the 1,150 national ZIP-row median, and 57 metros carry a 2BR median of 2,000 or more while 97 sit at 1,000 or below.

The ranking below groups the 51,895 ZIP rows by area_name and takes the median 2BR FMR across each metro's own rows. One method note: this cut weighs every ZIP equally, so a metro's median here can differ slightly from a median over unique FMR values, which is the cut the HUD metro area list shows when it sorts the same areas by ZIP count. What a "metro" even is in this file, MSA versus HUD Metro FMR Area, is the angle of HUD FMR by metro area.

The 15 priciest metros, by 2BR median

Eleven of the fifteen are in California, and the top four all are. Santa Cruz-Watsonville leads FY2026 with a 2BR median of 4,350, San Francisco and San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara tie at 3,480, and Santa Ana-Anaheim-Irvine closes the top four at 3,240. New York is the East Coast's entry at 3,175, its priciest ZIP reaching 4,370, and Boston takes the fifteenth spot at 2,690. Every 2BR low and high in the table is a real ZIP in that metro, so the range shows what Small Area FMRs do inside a single metro; SAFMR vs FMR explains the mechanism.

The 10 cheapest metros, by 2BR median

Puerto Rico owns the bottom ten outright. The 2BR medians run from 520 in Yauco, San Germán, and Guayama up to 610 in Fajardo, and even San Juan-Guaynabo, the island's priciest metro at 670, prices at barely half the national ZIP-row median of 1,150. The cheapest mainland entry is Morehouse Parish, LA at 855, still 64 percent above Yauco.

The bedroom-size gaps stretch with the ranking. Santa Cruz-Watsonville's studio median of 3,280 prices at more than four times the median 4BR in Yauco, 720, and its 4BR median of 5,850 runs 8.1 times it. A studio in the priciest metro rents for more than a 4-bedroom in the cheapest, which is the spread in its sharpest form. The bedroom-by-bedroom view of the same file is in HUD FMR by bedroom.

Why metros price so far apart

HUD sets each metro's FMR at the 40th percentile of gross rents in that area, so the number tracks the local rental market rather than a national benchmark. That design is why the spread is this wide: the 40th percentile in Santa Cruz-Watsonville reflects a coastal market of 23 ZIPs where the cheapest ZIP still carries a 2BR of 3,440, while the 40th percentile in Yauco reflects rents that top out at 540. Building limits do real work at the top of the table, where island and coastal geographies cap how much new supply can meet demand, and the top four slots are all California metros for exactly that reason.

For voucher households the metro number is the one that matters, because the payment standard follows it. The same Housing Choice Voucher budget covers a 2BR at the 40th percentile in Yauco or needs to stretch to 4,350 in Santa Cruz-Watsonville, which is why payment standards and the metro-level FMR move together. HUD FMR by state shows the same ranking one level up, HUD FMR by county one level down, and the single-ZIP lookup in HUD FMR by ZIP code resolves the exact block you care about.

Rank the metros yourself

The full 51,895-row ZIP file rebuilds both tables exactly: group by area_name, filter the metro column to metro areas, and take the median of fmr_2br per group. Over the dataset-mcp server, one query pulls every New York metro row for the group:

query_dataset(
  "hud-fmr-2026",
  {
    "where": [{ "column": "area_name", "op": "=", "value": "New York, NY HUD Metro FMR Area" }],
    "columns": ["zip", "area_name", "state", "fmr_2br"]
  }
)

Swap the where clause for any metro in either table. The naming conventions behind area_name, and the full 2,599-area breakdown, are in HUD metro area list.

HUD FMR by metro questions

Which metro area has the highest HUD FMR in FY2026?
Santa Cruz-Watsonville, CA tops the FY2026 metro ranking with a 2BR median of 4,350 dollars across its 23 ZIP rows, and its priciest ZIP reaches 5,230. San Francisco and San Jose-Sunnyvale-Santa Clara tie at 3,480, Santa Ana-Anaheim-Irvine follows at 3,240, and New York, NY rounds out the top five at 3,175. At the other end, Yauco, PR carries a 2BR median of 520, so the most expensive metro prices at 8.4 times the cheapest.
How many HUD metro areas are in the FY2026 FMR data?
The FY2026 ZIP-level file covers 2,599 distinct HUD FMR area names, of which 649 are metro areas; this page ranks those 649 by the median 2BR FMR across each metro's ZIP rows. HUD codes a few high-rent markets as nonmetro, so they fall outside the ranking: Nantucket County, MA carries a 2BR median of 3,140, which would rank sixth among metros, but its rows carry the nonmetro flag.
Why does HUD FMR differ so much between metros?
HUD sets a separate FMR for each metro area at the 40th percentile of gross rents in that area, so the number moves with the local rental market rather than with any national figure. In FY2026 the metro medians run from 520 dollars in Yauco, PR to 4,350 dollars in Santa Cruz-Watsonville, CA, an 8.4x spread that mirrors what local rents actually do. A voucher's payment standard follows the metro number, and in many metros HUD goes a step further with Small Area FMRs set per ZIP.
