HSA vs FSA 2026-2027: limits, run-out rules and how to spend the balance

Published 2026-09-12 by Matchbook Labs. Data source: IRS Publication 969, retrieved 2026-09-12; 2026 is its latest published year. Plan documents set the numbers you actually use, so this is a spending reference, not tax advice.

An HSA beats an FSA whenever you qualify: contributions, growth and withdrawals all go untaxed when the spending qualifies, and the balance survives every year end. A health FSA is the account that can take money back on January 1. The 2026 HSA limits are final, so this is arithmetic, not preference.

The flexible spending cap prints at $3,300 for 2025 with carryover capped at $660, and Publication 969 lists no 2026 figure. The IRS sets each plan year's ceiling in inflation guidance the November before, and employers may go lower.

Which account you can hold

The HSA gate is the health plan: a high deductible plan meeting both thresholds above, no other coverage that pays before the deductible, no Medicare. Fail in a single month and that month's contribution is an excess taxed at 6 percent a year until it leaves.

The FSA gate is your employer. You elect at open enrollment and the amount leaves every paycheck in equal slices, whatever your year looks like. Lose the job and the election stands: COBRA keeps an FSA alive at the remaining election plus 102 percent of the premium. One bridge runs between them: leave FSA coverage, become HSA-eligible, and a one-time tax-free distribution can move what is left.

Run-out season is now

September is when FSA balances get spent, because the forfeit deadline is close. A plan gives one relief option, or none:

  • Grace period. Up to 2 and a half months past the plan year, so a calendar-year plan pays last year's balance for care through March 15. The date of service must land inside the window.
  • Carryover. A capped amount moves into next year. Publication 969 puts the 2025 cap at $660.

A plan can run both, and that is where the arithmetic bites: carried money counts against the 2027 limit, so January's room comes in under the election. Work backward from your plan's deadline, or from the rent affordability calculator. Eligible spending runs wider than most assume: prescriptions, dental work, vision correction, first-aid supplies, over-the-counter medicine bought after 2019.

Worked example

Family coverage, 4,000 dollars in the HSA through payroll, a 2,200 dollar crowns quote in November. Add 4,750 dollars before December 31 and pay the dentist from it. Now a calendar-year FSA with 1,800 elected and 900 unspent on October 1: the rest of the year to find 900 dollars of care, and anything above the carryover cap stops being yours. Fund an FSA only to what you are certain to spend; max the HSA if you can carry the deductible.

Keeping the calendar straight

Three deadlines run at once when you hold both accounts: plan year end, the run-out claim cutoff, and the HSA contribution deadline, your filing date, not December 31. Frequently asked questions

Can I have an HSA and an FSA at the same time?
Not a general-purpose health FSA; it makes you ineligible for every month it covers you. A limited-purpose FSA, dental, vision and preventive only, sits beside an HSA.
Does unused FSA money expire on December 31?
It depends on the plan. A grace period can run 2 and a half months past the plan year, or a capped carryover moves money into next year. Your plan document says which. A fiscal-year plan ends when the plan says.
What happens to money left in an HSA at year end?
Nothing. It rolls forward without a limit, stays yours if you change jobs, and can be invested. A non-qualified withdrawal costs income tax plus 20 percent under 65.
Have the 2027 limits been announced?
Not in the current edition of Publication 969, which ends at 2026. Plan on 4,400 dollars self-only and 8,750 dollars family, then check the IRS inflation guidance when 2027 figures land.
