HUD FMR 2027: what the new fair market rents changed, ZIP by ZIP

FY2027 edition, published 2026-09-10. Built from HUD's FY2027 workbooks, downloaded from huduser.gov on 2026-09-08 and SHA-256 verified. Source data public domain. Data Vault is not affiliated with HUD. This page documents the data; it is not housing or legal advice.

The short answer

FY2027 is live. HUD posted FY27_FMRs.xlsx on August 26, 2026 and the ZIP-level fy2027_safmrs.xlsx on August 31, 2026, and the new rates take effect October 1, 2026. We parsed both workbooks into the same ten-column CSV shape as the FY2026 file on the same day: 51,871 ZIP rows, a 3,229-row county rollup, and a 52-row state summary, published free on Zenodo under DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22649503.

The headline number: the median 2BR change across the file is +3.30%. That average hides a spread from about -10% at the bottom to +70.37% at the top, so this page walks the year-over-year diff the way you would actually run it: matched ZIP areas, one lookup side by side, and the outliers both directions. Every number below was computed from the two CSVs, not copied from a summary.

What changed from FY2026 to FY2027

Join fmr-by-zip-2026.csv and fmr-by-zip-2027.csv on zip plus hud_area_code and 51,819 of the FY2027 rows match a FY2026 row. Of those, 51,800 changed in at least one bedroom size. At the 2BR line, 36,101 matched areas went up, 13,885 went down, and 1,833 held flat. The median of the matched 2BR changes is +3.30%, and the median 2BR level itself moved from 1,150 to 1,190.

State medians show the same mix. From the 52-row state summaries, the 2BR median went up in New York (1,430 to 1,500), Texas (1,190 to 1,220), Alabama (980 to 1,020), and Colorado (1,620 to 1,650), and down in Arizona (1,610 to 1,520) and California (2,635 to 2,600). Alaska held flat at 1,510. The ZIP-level counts at the top and bottom of the range tell you where the sharp moves sit:

The biggest move: Kingston, NY, +70.37%

ZIP 12461 sits in the Kingston, NY MSA. Its full rows, verbatim from both files:

12461,METRO28740M28740,metro,"Kingston, NY MSA",NY,1240,1240,1620,2060,2220
12461,METRO28740M28740,metro,"Kingston, NY MSA",NY,1920,2110,2760,3350,3630

First line FY2026, second line FY2027, same ten columns. Every bedroom size jumped: the 2BR went 1,620 to 2,760 (+70.37%), the 0BR 1,240 to 1,920, the 4BR 2,220 to 3,630. Neighboring Kingston-area ZIPs moved far less, 1,870 to 2,030 in ZIP 12401 and 1,820 to 1,880 in ZIP 12402, so the new value is ZIP-specific rather than a metro-wide reset. Large single-ZIP jumps like this are what Small Area FMRs produce; SAFMR vs FMR explains the mechanism and HUD's designation rule.

At the other end, the steepest cuts in the file all land at exactly -10.00% (ZIPs 30331 in Atlanta, 78655 in San Antonio, 06824 in Bridgeport among them), which reads like a floor rather than a coincidence. If your numbers matter for a program decision, check the specific ZIP against the official workbook before you act on the diff.

Run the year-over-year diff yourself

The whole median calculation is a few lines of csv-module Python over the two files:

import csv, statistics

rows26, rows27 = {}, {}
for path, store in [("fmr-by-zip-2026.csv", rows26), ("fmr-by-zip-2027.csv", rows27)]:
    with open(path, newline="", encoding="utf-8") as f:
        for row in csv.DictReader(f):
            store[(row["zip"], row["hud_area_code"])] = int(row["fmr_2br"])

common = set(rows26) & set(rows27)
changes = [(rows27[k] - rows26[k]) / rows26[k] * 100 for k in common]
print(len(common), round(statistics.median(changes), 2))
# 51819 3.3

Both files keep the zip column as five-character text, so leading zeros survive; the loading details, including the dtype={'zip': str} habit, are in FMR dataset download. Where each year's numbers come from, and how HUD's workbooks compare with Census Reporter and the CSV route, is the subject of HUD FMR data download.

Where to get the FY2027 data

Huduser.gov is the primary source: FY27_FMRs.xlsx for the county-level values and fy2027_safmrs.xlsx for the ZIP-level ones. Both are Excel workbooks with the usual parse hazards, hard line breaks in headers and ZIPs that want to lose their leading zeros. The cleaned versions of exactly those files are on Zenodo under DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22649503: all three CSVs plus the data dictionary and build script, CC BY 4.0, citable and free.

Two honest status notes. The dataset-mcp server still serves the FY2026 file (query_dataset("hud-fmr-2026", ...), 51,895 rows); the FY2027 CSVs land in it with the FY2027 pack release. Until then, FY2027 means downloading the Zenodo CSVs. And for anything dated before October 1, 2026, the HUD FMR FY2026 pack remains the right file, with its 51,895-row ZIP dataset and free 22-row sample. If the benchmark itself is the question, what is fair market rent explains the 40th percentile rule, the fiscal-year cycle, and how the payment-standard band follows each year's FMR.

HUD FMR 2027 questions

When do the 2027 fair market rents take effect?
October 1, 2026. HUD posted the FY2027 county workbook (FY27_FMRs.xlsx) on August 26, 2026 and the ZIP-level SAFMR workbook (fy2027_safmrs.xlsx) on August 31, 2026. Those files are the operating basis for voucher payment standards in the new federal fiscal year; the FY2026 numbers stop being the current rates the same day.
How much did fair market rents change for 2027?
Most areas moved a few percent. Matching 51,819 ZIP-level areas present in both years, 51,800 changed in at least one bedroom size, and the median 2BR change is +3.30%. Moves ran both ways: 36,101 matched areas up and 13,885 down at the 2BR line, with the steepest cuts near -10% in ZIPs of the Atlanta, San Antonio, and Bridgeport metros. The largest jump is ZIP 12461 in the Kingston, NY MSA, where the 2BR FMR went from 1,620 to 2,760, +70.37%.
Where can I download the 2027 FMR data?
Two places. HUD publishes the official workbooks on huduser.gov: FY27_FMRs.xlsx at the county level and fy2027_safmrs.xlsx with one row per ZIP area. The same data parsed into clean CSVs is on Zenodo under DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22649503: fmr-by-zip-2027.csv (51,871 rows), a 3,229-row county rollup, and a 52-row state summary, CC BY 4.0, no signup.
