Where HUD fair market rent peaks and bottoms out, ZIP by ZIP, FY2027

FY2027 edition, published 2026-09-10. Data source: HUD FY2027 Fair Market Rent release, ZIP-level file parsed from FY27_FMRs.xlsx plus the FY2027 small-area FMR file (FY27_safmrs.xlsx), rates effective October 1, 2026. The short answer

HUD's FY2027 ZIP-level file prices the 2BR fair market rent for 51,871 ZIP-and-area rows, and the two ends of it sit 4,370 dollars apart. The ceiling is ZIP 95060 in the Santa Cruz-Watsonville, California metro at 5,260 dollars, 5.9 times the 890-dollar floor shared by 173 rows across 133 ZIP codes in Louisiana parishes and South Carolina counties. Between the ends, the middle 80 percent of ranked rows run from 960 to 2,210 dollars around a 1,190-dollar median. The top of the board belongs to two places: five of the ten most expensive rows carry Santa Cruz-Watsonville rates and five carry San Francisco small-area rates, all ten in California. One region owns the whole bottom: every floor row is a Louisiana parish or a South Carolina county.

The ZIP cut reaches past the county cut. Santa Cruz County's 2BR median in the FY2027 county file is 4,254 dollars, the priciest county median in the nation, while the 95060 rate inside that same county runs 5,260, a 1,006-dollar premium that county medians smooth away. Pull Puerto Rico's 216 rows out with the territories and 51,655 rows remain for the rankings; keep them and ZIP 00669 in Aguadilla takes the bottom at 520 dollars. Both boards recompute from the CSV behind the FY2027 ZIP dataset, and the two SQL blocks at the end of this page return the tables verbatim.

The board splits five and five. ZIPs 95060, 95062, 95065, 95003 and 95017 carry Santa Cruz-Watsonville rates from 4,700 to 5,260 dollars; 94404, 94107, 94158, 94925 and 94123 carry San Francisco rates from 4,650 to 5,050. Exactly ten rows sit at 4,650 dollars or above, so this is the complete club rather than a cut-off sample, and 95060 is the only row at 5,260. ZIPs 94107 and 94158 tie at 4,930 under one small-area schedule, and the metro's 94404 rate beats both even though San Francisco's own 94123 rounds out the list 280 dollars lower.

The area code tells you where each rent comes from. The first five digits after METRO or NCNTY carry the CBSA or area number: 42100 is the Santa Cruz-Watsonville CBSA, so its rows read METRO42100M42100, and 41860 is San Francisco's, so its rows read METRO41860MM7360. Trailing digits pin the sub-area, county FIPS included: NCNTY22101N22101 is St. Mary Parish, Louisiana, FIPS 22101 on both sides. One area code can carry many rents. The 113 San Francisco rows hold 50 distinct 2BR values from 3,250 to 5,050 dollars, and Santa Cruz's 23 rows hold 16 from 3,300 to 5,260. Variation is the rule across the file: every one of the 460 metro area codes with an M or MM schedule carries more than one 2BR value, 2,577 of the 2,600 area codes do overall, and the 23 exceptions are mostly nonmetro counties with a handful of ZIPs.

The rung above the floor sits 10 dollars up, 900 dollars, carried by 45 rows, so the cheapest non-floor rent in the ranked file is a rounding-error climb from the bottom.

The middle band and the state spreads

Percentiles frame the whole distribution. The 10th percentile of ranked 2BR rents is 960 dollars, the median 1,190, the 90th percentile 2,210, a 1,250-dollar span for the middle 80 percent. State by state the spread widens where the coast is. New York runs from 1,060 at the 10th percentile to 3,270 at the 90th, a 2,210-dollar gap, the widest in the file, and its 90th percentile prices at 3.08 times its 10th. California follows at 1,470 to 3,590 (2,120 dollars), Massachusetts at 1,790 to 3,194 (1,404), Maryland at 1,140 to 2,540 (1,400) and Washington at 1,240 to 2,600 (1,360). Next come Florida (1,340), Colorado (1,320) and New Jersey (1,190).

The tight end stretches barely a fifth as far. West Virginia spans 940 to 1,150 dollars, a 210-dollar gap, with Arkansas at 240 (910 to 1,150) and Kansas at 250 (960 to 1,210). DC shows what small-area schedules do inside one jurisdiction: its 488 rows all share the DC-VA-MD HUD Metro FMR Area code, and the percentiles still run 1,837 to 3,006 dollars, a 1,169-dollar spread, because the area prices ZIP by ZIP. Ranking each ZIP's single highest schedule instead of every row leaves the story intact: the top five values belong to the same five ZIPs at the same rents, and the 133 floor ZIPs are the same 133.

Methodology: rows, areas, and exclusions

The source is HUD's FY2027 ZIP-level fair market rent file, 51,871 rows parsed from FY27_FMRs.xlsx plus the FY2027 small-area FMR file (FY27_safmrs.xlsx), rates effective October 1, 2026. The published copy is byte-identical to that parse, sha256-checked at build time, public domain. Rankings include the 50 states and DC; Puerto Rico's 216 rows are the file's only territory rows and stay out, the treatment the county-level pages extend to all five territories. Puerto Rico's own 2BR rates run 520 to 1,060 dollars, so the file-wide floor, 520 dollars at ZIP 00669 in Aguadilla, never appears in the tables above.

One row is one ZIP code under one HUD area, and 10,093 of the file's 38,601 ZIP codes carry more than one row, which is why the floor table repeats two ZIPs and why row counts can exceed ZIP counts. Percentiles interpolate linearly between the two nearest order statistics, the numpy default. FMRs are HUD's 40th-percentile rent estimates rather than observed asking rents, HUD can revise FY2027 mid-cycle, and rows of one area share a schedule, so a block of identical values reflects one area's rent stamped onto its ZIPs. This page documents the data; it is not housing or legal advice.

Recompute the boards yourself

Load the FY2027 ZIP file into SQLite as zips_fy2027, ten columns per row, following the import steps in the SQL guide. The two boards are one ORDER BY each:

-- SQLite: the ten most expensive 2BR ZIP rows, FY2027
CREATE TABLE zips_fy2027 (zip TEXT, hud_area_code TEXT, metro TEXT,
  area_name TEXT, state TEXT, fmr_0br INT, fmr_1br INT,
  fmr_2br INT, fmr_3br INT, fmr_4br INT);
.import --skip 1 fmr-by-zip-2027.csv zips_fy2027

SELECT zip, hud_area_code, area_name, state, fmr_2br
FROM zips_fy2027
WHERE state <> 'PR'
ORDER BY fmr_2br DESC, state, zip, hud_area_code
LIMIT 10;
-- Rows: 95060 METRO42100M42100 Santa Cruz-Watsonville CA 5260, 95062 METRO42100M42100 5100,
-- 94404 METRO41860MM7360 San Francisco CA 5050, 95065 METRO42100M42100 4980,
-- 94107 METRO41860MM7360 4930, 94158 METRO41860MM7360 4930, 95003 METRO42100M42100 4920,
-- 94925 METRO41860MM7360 4710, 95017 METRO42100M42100 4700, 94123 METRO41860MM7360 4650.

-- the ten cheapest, same table, order flipped
SELECT zip, hud_area_code, area_name, state, fmr_2br
FROM zips_fy2027
WHERE state <> 'PR'
ORDER BY fmr_2br ASC, state, zip, hud_area_code
LIMIT 10;
-- Rows: 70342 NCNTY22101N22101 St. Mary Parish LA 890, 70426 NCNTY22117N22117 Washington Parish 890,
-- 70429 NCNTY22117N22117 890, 70526 METRO29180N22001 Acadia Parish 890,

The shape behind both boards, national percentiles, the floor block, and the state spreads, comes out of the same table:

-- SQLite: national percentiles with linear interpolation
WITH p AS (
  SELECT fmr_2br AS v, ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY fmr_2br) AS rn,
         COUNT(*) OVER () AS n
  FROM zips_fy2027
  WHERE state <> 'PR'
)
SELECT
  ROUND((SELECT a.v + (0.10*(c.n-1) - CAST(0.10*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER)) * (b.v - a.v)
         FROM p c, p a, p b
         WHERE a.rn = CAST(0.10*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 1
           AND b.rn = CAST(0.10*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 2 LIMIT 1), 2) AS p10,
  ROUND((SELECT a.v + (0.50*(c.n-1) - CAST(0.50*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER)) * (b.v - a.v)
         FROM p c, p a, p b
         WHERE a.rn = CAST(0.50*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 1
           AND b.rn = CAST(0.50*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 2 LIMIT 1), 2) AS p50,
  ROUND((SELECT a.v + (0.90*(c.n-1) - CAST(0.90*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER)) * (b.v - a.v)
         FROM p c, p a, p b
         WHERE a.rn = CAST(0.90*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 1
           AND b.rn = CAST(0.90*(c.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 2 LIMIT 1), 2) AS p90;
-- 960.0 / 1190.0 / 2210.0.

-- the floor block: rows, ZIPs, areas, and the two states
SELECT COUNT(*) AS floor_rows, COUNT(DISTINCT zip) AS floor_zips,
       COUNT(DISTINCT area_name) AS areas,
       SUM(state = 'LA') AS la_rows, SUM(state = 'SC') AS sc_rows
FROM zips_fy2027
WHERE state <> 'PR'
  AND fmr_2br = (SELECT MIN(fmr_2br) FROM zips_fy2027 WHERE state <> 'PR');
-- 173 / 133 / 50 / 114 / 59.

-- state spreads, widest first (same interpolation, partitioned by state)
WITH p AS (
  SELECT state, fmr_2br AS v,
         ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY state ORDER BY fmr_2br) AS rn,
         COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY state) AS n
  FROM zips_fy2027
  WHERE state <> 'PR'
),
states AS (SELECT DISTINCT state, n FROM p)
SELECT s.state, s.n AS zip_rows,
  ROUND((SELECT a.v + (0.10*(s.n-1) - CAST(0.10*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER)) * (b.v - a.v)
         FROM p a, p b
         WHERE a.state = s.state AND b.state = s.state
           AND a.rn = CAST(0.10*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 1
           AND b.rn = CAST(0.10*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 2), 2) AS p10,
  ROUND((SELECT a.v + (0.50*(s.n-1) - CAST(0.50*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER)) * (b.v - a.v)
         FROM p a, p b
         WHERE a.state = s.state AND b.state = s.state
           AND a.rn = CAST(0.50*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 1
           AND b.rn = CAST(0.50*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 2), 2) AS p50,
  ROUND((SELECT a.v + (0.90*(s.n-1) - CAST(0.90*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER)) * (b.v - a.v)
         FROM p a, p b
         WHERE a.state = s.state AND b.state = s.state
           AND a.rn = CAST(0.90*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 1
           AND b.rn = CAST(0.90*(s.n-1) AS INTEGER) + 2), 2) AS p90
FROM states s
ORDER BY p90 - p10 DESC
LIMIT 8;
-- NY 2397 1060.0 1500.0 3270.0, CA 2605 1470.0 2600.0 3590.0, MA 767 1790.0 2550.0 3194.0,
-- MD 389 1140.0 1720.0 2540.0, WA 760 1240.0 1620.0 2600.0, FL 1552 1220.0 1820.0 2560.0,
-- CO 771 1110.0 1650.0 2430.0, NJ 641 1920.0 2390.0 3110.0.

Run in one session, both blocks return exactly the rows quoted in the tables and comments above. Swap fmr_2br for fmr_1br to re-rank the ladder one rung down, or drop the WHERE state <> 'PR' filter to watch 00669 take the bottom of the low board at 520 dollars.

The 2BR extremes, FY2027: questions

Which ZIP code has the highest HUD fair market rent in FY2027?
ZIP 95060 in the Santa Cruz-Watsonville, California MSA tops the file at 5,260 dollars for a 2BR, with 95062 next at 5,100 and San Francisco's 94404 at 5,050. The ten most expensive rows all sit in California, between 4,650 and 5,260 dollars: five carry Santa Cruz-Watsonville rates and five carry San Francisco small-area rates, and everything at 4,650 or above makes the board.

At 890 dollars, shared by 173 rows across 133 ZIP codes in Louisiana parishes (114 rows) and South Carolina counties (59 rows). ZIP 70342 in St. Mary Parish, Louisiana leads the tie-break order, Washington Parish lands two ZIPs, and ZIP 70526 carries both an Acadia Parish and a Vermilion Parish row at the floor. How were the ZIP extremes computed?
