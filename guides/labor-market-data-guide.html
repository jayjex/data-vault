Eurostat unemployment data: the EU labour-market dataset, ready to download

Eurostat publishes the EU's official unemployment numbers in table une_rt_a ("Unemployment by sex and age - annual data"), harmonised to the ILO labour-force survey definition so rates compare across member states. Downloading it from the source means the Eurostat dissemination API or a multi-sheet TSV export; both need parsing before the numbers land in one table. This guide starts from the parsed result: one flat CSV with 3,768 rows covering the EU-27 aggregate, the euro area, and eight countries, annual rates from 2003 to 2025, both sexes plus male/female splits, and seven age bands from Y15-24 to Y55-74. The full file is a free download below, no key and no registration.

Ten geos, one CSV

The file carries two aggregates and eight countries. Every row is a percentage of the labour force (unit PC_ACT), and the table below shows the 2025 headline rate for each geo (age Y15-74, both sexes) next to where that series started. France goes back to 2003 and Sweden to 2005; the rest start in 2009, because the EU-27 aggregate and most national series in this slice only begin once every member state's data exists.

What the numbers show

The EU-27 headline rate sat at 6.0% in 2025, half its 2013 crisis peak of 11.6%. Spread across the eight countries: Poland lowest at 3.1%, Spain highest at 10.5%, and Spain is the only one of the eight still above 10%. The sex split runs slightly female-heavy in 2025 (EU-27 female 6.2% vs male 5.8%), and the gap held through most of the series.

Youth tells a sharper story. In 2013, the worst year in the file, Greek youth (Y15-24) unemployment hit 64.6% for women and 59.2% for both sexes combined; Spain's youth rate reached 55.5% that same year. By 2025 the EU-27 youth rate is down to 15.2%, though Sweden (24.3%) and Spain (24.9%) still run youth rates above one in five. Italy's youth peak landed a year after the EU aggregate, 42.7% in 2014, worth remembering when you sort by year instead of eyeballing 2013.

One structural note for anyone charting trends: a few countries recovered before the aggregates. Germany's headline rate peaked back in 2009 at 4.9% and fell from there, so filtering the CSV to 2013-only troughs hides the German case. The Netherlands halved from 8.2% (2013) to 3.9% (2025); Poland went 10.6% to 3.1% over the same stretch.

Columns in the file

Eight columns per row: year (2003-2025), geo (EU27, EA20, or an ISO 2-letter country code), sex (T / F / M), age (the band code), value (the rate in percent), plus Eurostat's own labels for geo, sex, and age. Missing cells are absent rows, not nulls, so a pivot on year × geo with a headline filter yields 17 series points for most countries and 23 for France. The schema.md file next to the CSV documents the caveats: break-in-series flags exist in Eurostat's TSV export but not in this JSON-stat-derived file, and annual rates need no seasonal adjustment.

Pair it with the housing files

Labour and housing cycle together, and the other two Eurostat files in this catalog cover the housing side: the house price index (HPI) dataset (342 rows, NL + EU-27, three unit variants) and the HICP actual rents dataset. Both read the same indexes the HPI page works through: the Netherlands and EU-27 start at 100.0 in 2015, and by 2025 Dutch prices sit at 215.15 against the EU aggregate's 161.93. Join key is the same in all three files (year × geo), so a two-line pandas merge lines up Spanish youth unemployment against Spanish price growth. For US rent context, the HUD FMR dataset covers the same question at ZIP level.
