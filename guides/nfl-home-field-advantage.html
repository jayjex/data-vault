NFL home field advantage by the numbers: 27 seasons, 7,182 home games

Counted from the NFL Games & Betting Lines 1999-2026 pack, file games.csv, 7,548 rows · per-team season records behind every split: the NFL stats by team guide · where the raw file comes from and what else it carries: NFL data APIs compared. Nothing on this page is betting advice.

What we counted, and what got excluded

Home field advantage has an exact definition in a game file: the extra win probability and scoring margin that shows up when one side is the designated home team. The pack's games.csv holds 7,548 NFL games from the 1999 season through the 2026 schedule. Of those, 7,276 have final scores. The location column marks 94 of the played games as neutral-site, Super Bowls and international series among them, where the "home" label is an accounting fiction, so they drop out. That leaves 7,182 home-site games, and every number below is a count over those rows.

The headline: home teams went 4,056-3,112-14, a 56.5% win rate. They also outscored visitors 23.32 to 20.93 points per game on average. That 2.39-point scoring gap is the raw material the betting market turns into a line, which we check later in the page.

Two things stand out in the third era. Cut it finer and 2016-2019 still ran at 56.9% (596-447-4 in 1,047 games), right on the previous era. Then 2020 happened: with stadiums empty or near-empty, home teams went 133-131-1, 50.2%, which is the closest any season in the file has come to a losing record at home. Crowd noise was doing real work. The rate recovered only partway after crowds returned, 54.4% across 2021-2026 (757-631-4 in 1,392 games), and the last four seasons sit at 57.0%, 56.6%, 53.6%, and 53.4% from 2022 through 2025.

One more constant worth knowing: across all 27 seasons in the file, no full season ended with home teams under .500. Even 2020 finished one game over. The advantage shrank from 58.2% toward 55.0%, but it has never flipped sign over a whole year in this dataset.

Dome vs outdoor: the gap that isn't there

Roof type is a column in the file (roof: dome, outdoors, open, closed), so the stadium-advantage question gets a direct count:

Dome teams win at home at almost exactly the same rate as outdoor teams, 56.8% vs 56.7%. The weather-free environment buys a home crowd and a familiar surface, and the numbers say that nets out to a tenth of a point. Dome advantage did slide across eras like the league-wide number did, 60.1% in 1999-2005, 54.6% in 2006-2015, 56.1% in 2016-2026, so the dome edge of the early 2000s is mostly gone.

What the closing spread thinks of all this

The file carries spread_line, the closing point spread with the home team's sign convention, on all 7,182 home-site games. The market prices home field in outright: home teams were favored in 4,692 of them (65.3%), visitors in 2,459, with 31 pick'ems, and the average line was +2.30 for the home side. That number tracks the 2.39-point scoring gap from the first section almost exactly. Bookmakers charge for the jersey.

Read together: the win rate is real, 56.5%, and the market knows it, yet home teams covered the number only 47.7% of the time. The line eats the advantage. Home underdogs actually covered slightly more often than home favorites, 50.5% vs 48.3%, the same near-coin-flip the spread history page finds league-wide, and the point spread history guide tracks the same pattern across era averages, record 20-point lines, and the biggest upsets. That is the honest summary of nfl home win rate data against the number: good for winning games, not for beating an efficient price.

What these counts cannot tell you

The file records final scores, locations, roofs, and closing lines. It has no attendance figures, no injury reports, no travel data, and no probability of any future result. The 2020 collapse to 50.2% is consistent with crowd effects, but the data cannot separate crowd from travel from randomness on its own. Era splits describe completed games; they are a baseline for research questions like "did the dome edge really vanish after 2005" or "which franchises beat the league home rate most often", both one filter away. Nothing here predicts the next kickoff, and none of it is a recommendation to bet one.

Home field advantage questions

What is the NFL home win rate all-time?

Counted from the games CSV, home-site NFL teams went 4,056-3,112-14 across the 7,182 played games from 1999 through 2026 where one side was the designated home team, a .565 win rate, 56.5%. Home teams also outscored visitors 23.32 to 20.93 points per game on average, a gap of 2.39 points. Neutral-site games (94, mostly Super Bowls and internationals) are excluded from these counts.

Is home field advantage in the NFL shrinking?

The file says yes. Home teams won 58.2% of 1,836 home-site games from 1999 to 2005, 56.9% of 2,642 from 2006 to 2015, and 55.0% of 2,704 from 2016 through 2026. The average home margin fell in step, from 2.98 points to 2.43 to 1.96. The sharpest single-year dip was 2020, when empty stadiums cut the home win rate to 50.2% (133-131-1), the closest any season came to a losing record at home in 27 seasons; no full season in the file has one.

Do dome teams win more at home?

Barely, league-wide. Home teams won 56.8% of 1,180 dome games against 56.7% of 5,266 outdoor games since 1999, a difference of a tenth of a point. Per team the splits swing much wider: Minnesota won 65.2% of its dome home games, Indianapolis 73.4%, while Detroit went 47.5% (105-116) in its dome and Las Vegas 41.2% (21-30) since 2020. The roof matters less than the franchise; the top-5 dome table above shows each team's dome and outdoor rates side by side.
