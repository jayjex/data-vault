3D printed desk organizers and printable tools: 10 parametric models

Published 2026-09-10. All models on the MakerWorld @jayjey profile, license CC BY 4.0. Page by Data Vault.

Ten parametric 3D printing models, generated as code and uploaded to our MakerWorld profile. The set covers desk organizers and printable tools: an adhesive cable clip set, a two-piece phone stand, a wall-mount bit holder, two rulers, a bag clip, a hex key gauge, and a few household helpers. One model is live on MakerWorld and nine sat in MakerWorld's review queue when this page was published, so their model pages will appear as review clears. Everything here prints flat on the plate with no supports, and every generator ships with the model files so you can rebuild any part at your own dimensions.

One thing to know before you download: these are renders and mesh-checked geometry, not test prints. Each mesh passed watertight and manifold checks before upload, but no filament has run through them yet. Print at your own settings, and the model descriptions on MakerWorld say the same thing.

What parametric means here

Each model ships with its generator script in the files. The cable clip generator takes cable diameter, wall thickness, clearance, and wall height. The seed ruler takes length, both notch pitches, and notch depth. The hex gauge takes the size list and clearance. Change the parameters, rerun the script, and you get a rebuilt STL and 3MF sized for your cables, drawer, or seed packets. Every mesh that came off these generators was checked watertight and manifold before upload. The parametric tools guide lists each model's rebuildable parameters side by side. Printed dimensions and material volume for the clip sizes are in the cable clip STL spec sheet. The stand also has a fold-flat sibling that hinges in place instead of separating: its pin-hinge geometry and measured volume are in the phone stand STL spec sheet. The ruler in row 3 has its own measured layout breakdown, tick profile and stencil cutout sizes in the ruler stencil STL spec sheet. Outside the ten, a newer model measures map routes instead of drawers: its thread ratio, per-part sizes and volume are in the opisometer STL spec sheet. Also outside the ten, a bench tool for the biology side: a one-piece block with 24/16/8 mm work stages and Ø1.5 mm pin guide holes, measured in the insect pinning block STL spec sheet. A second, compact version of that block sits on a 6 mm base with step tops at 30, 22 and 14 mm and a Ø0.65 mm guide hole matched to #3 pins; its own numbers are in the compact pinning block STL spec sheet. For the bench hardware side, an eight-bay dock that parks loose hotend nozzles tip-up in two rows of four, front MK8 and back V6, with no supports needed: nozzle dock STL spec sheet. And for the night-sky side, a three-part planisphere for 45°N: 416 star holes sized by magnitude, a true-horizon window, a barbed snap-pin pivot and a snap-on hour ring, measured in the planisphere STL spec sheet. Newest in the pool, for the classroom side: a print-in-place counting frame where 100 beads print loose inside sealed channels split 5+5 by a center rib, with the bead clearances verified against the exported mesh in the abacus STL spec sheet. On the workshop bench side, a one-piece cradle that parks two 180 × 60 × 15 mm whetstones side by side on a shared base, with the channel fit ray-tested on the exported mesh in the stone holder STL spec sheet. For the shelf itself, a pair of L bookends prints from one file: four blind pockets under each foot take 10 mm rubber dots so the pair grips the shelf, and the lattice cutouts are through-holes only; the pocket layout and slot column are measured in the bookend STL spec sheet. For the tool drawer, a socket size tray stands 1/4 inch drive metric sockets head-up in nine blind pockets, one per size 5-13 mm, each bore cut to across-flats + 5 mm so only the matching socket seats, with the size digits debossed into the front wall; the pocket widths and floor are ray-scanned in the socket tray STL spec sheet. Newest of all, a desk caddy that sorts pens three ways: a 44 x 27.5 mm front slot, an 11 mm pencil rail that keeps one visible row, and a 47 x 58 mm tall slot for markers or scissors, with the slot layout and the solid strip between the left slots measured in the pen organizer STL spec sheet.

Flat prints, no supports

Every part in the set sits flat on the print plate. Walls are vertical, cutouts open upward or straight through, and nothing overhangs, so there is no support material and no cleanup pass. The cable clip plate weighs about 2.5 g in PLA; the hex key gauge comes to 17.4 cm³ solid. Wall widths target a standard 0.4 mm nozzle: the clips run 2.4 mm walls, which is three perimeters.
