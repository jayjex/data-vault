3D printable education models: math manipulatives for class and home

Published 2026-09-10. All models land on the MakerWorld @jayjey profile, license CC BY 4.0. Page by Data Vault.

Four education models for the classroom and the specimen bench: a print-in-place abacus with 100 loose beads, a number line that splits into ten decade tiles, and two insect pinning blocks that hold pinned specimens and labels at fixed heights. If you are after math manipulatives to 3D print, the abacus and the number line are the two to start with. Each one was generated by a script and ships with that generator in the download, so rows, tiles, stage heights, and clearances are all rebuildable parameters. Each part prints flat on the plate with no supports.

One thing to know before you download: these are renders and mesh-checked geometry, not test prints. Every mesh passed watertight and manifold checks before upload, and the model descriptions say the same. The four models were staged as MakerWorld drafts when this page was published and had not cleared the daily submission limit yet, so their pages appear on the @jayjey profile as they go through.

The abacus: print-in-place beads, no stringing

Traditional abacus builds string beads on rods, which means assembly and, in 3D printing, printed rods that snap or sag. This one seals the beads in: each of the ten rows is a closed channel with a center rib, the beads print loose inside the channel, and the lid bridges the 8.3 mm gaps between ribs. Clearances are 0.3 mm above the floor, 0.35 mm under the lid, and 0.4 mm at the sides, so the beads slide but stay put. Because one half of a row holds exactly 5 beads, half a slide is 5, a full row is 10, and five rows are 50, which keeps place-value demos legible from a desk away.

The frame comes to about 160 cm³ solid, roughly 195 g of PLA, with an estimated 6-8 hours at 0.2 mm layers. The generator takes rows, beads per row, bead size, clearances, and wall and floor thickness as parameters. The full measured breakdown, including the 100-body bead check on the exported mesh, is in the abacus STL spec sheet.

The number line: decade tiles you can pop off

Each tile covers one decade, 0-9 through 90-99, with a tick and a raised numeral per unit. Ticks on multiples of 5 run taller, so counting by fives and tens is visible without color, which matters for classrooms with a single-extruder printer. Tiles join left to right with two tabs into open notches at 0.2 mm clearance, and that fit is loose on purpose: the tabs align flat tiles, they don't lock. The line runs in one row, snakes into two, or wraps around a desk corner, and you can pop off the decade a student is working on instead of pointing across a full meter of plastic.

All ten tiles fit one 256 mm plate in a 2×5 grid, about 126 cm³ of PLA for the sheet, roughly 156 g, with an estimated 4-6 hours at 0.2 mm layers. The generator takes decade start and count, unit pitch, tile size, numeral height, tick sizes, and tab clearances as parameters. The measured breakdown, including the ten-tile watertight check and the tab clearance numbers off the mesh, is in the number line STL spec sheet.

Two pinning blocks, two workflows

The three-stage block works where you set specimens onto labels. Push the pin down until it bottoms out on the stage you chose, 24 mm for the specimen, 16 mm for the first label, 8 mm for the second, and the pin stays straight in the 1.5 mm guide hole while you adjust. The tall stage also holds three spare pins point-down in 3 mm pockets, and the stage heights print as recessed numerals so the block reads at a glance.

The stepped block stages specimens at a uniform height across a whole drawer. Its holes run through to the base, so the pin tip rests on the bench and nothing rides on the hole bottom; step tops at 30/22/14 mm leave 24/16/8 mm of shaft above the block with standard 38 mm #3 pins. The default guide hole is 0.65 mm for 0.5 mm pin shanks; if pins grab, ream it with a spare pin or bump the clearance parameter and reprint. It is one solid piece, about 32 cm³ solid or 10-15 g of PLA at typical infill, and prints in under an hour.

Print notes

Every part sits flat on the plate with no supports. The abacus lid bridges its rib gaps; the number line tiles and both pinning blocks have vertical walls and upward or straight-through openings, so no overhangs anywhere. The hour and weight figures above are geometry estimates from the meshes, not measured prints, and the model descriptions carry the same wording.
