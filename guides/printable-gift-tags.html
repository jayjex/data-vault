3D printed gift tags: two sizes, TO and FROM engraved, no supports

Published 2026-09-10. Model on the MakerWorld @jayjey profile, license CC BY 4.0. Page by Data Vault.

A paper gift tag goes in the bin with the wrapping paper. A 3d printed gift tag comes back out of the ribbon drawer every year. This set puts two tags in one file: 70 × 40 mm and 50 × 28 mm, both 3 mm thick, in the classic clipped-corner shape. TO and FROM are engraved 0.8 mm into the face and the space between them stays blank, so every tag gets a handwritten name and can be reused after the names come off.

September is the month to print them. Halloween opens the gift season, and from there through Christmas a gift tag that survives ribbon, tape, and a crowded wrapping table earns its shelf space. The whole layout fits in 123 × 40 × 3 mm and uses 11.6 cm³ of PLA, so a full plate of tags comes off the printer in one sitting.

One honesty note up front: this set exists as renders and mesh-checked geometry, and it has not been test printed. Both meshes passed watertight and manifold checks before the upload went into MakerWorld's review queue.

Why the face stays blank

The TO and FROM lettering is cut 0.8 mm into the surface, deep enough to read clearly but shallow enough that the tags still print flat on the plate with nothing to support. Everything between the two words is open space, so the tag works for any recipient and any occasion. The 4.5 mm hole sits near the clipped corner with 5.7 to 6.6 mm of material around it, which keeps thin spots from forming where the string pulls.

Regenerate the tags at your own dimensions

The generator script ships inside the model's downloadable files, the same convention as the rest of our parametric printable tools. Change the tag count and sizes, corner radius, corner cut, hole diameter, text list, text height, or engraving depth, rerun the script, and it outputs a fresh watertight STL and 3MF with a mesh check printed as it finishes. Want 90 mm luggage tags or a four-word engraving? Those are parameter edits, not CAD work.

Where to get the files

The tag set was in MakerWorld's review queue when this page was published; its model page goes live as the review clears, and everything lands on the @jayjey profile. Each download includes the generator script, and CC BY 4.0 covers remixing with attribution.
