Parametric 3D printed tools: 10 models you can rebuild at your own dimensions

Published 2026-09-10. All models on the MakerWorld @jayjey profile, license CC BY 4.0. Page by Data Vault.

Every tool in this set was generated by a script, not modeled by hand, and every generator ships inside the model's downloadable files. That is what parametric means here: the STL and 3MF you download are one output of a program whose inputs are the dimensions. If our cable clip doesn't fit your cable or our ruler is the wrong length, you don't need CAD skills to fix it. You change numbers like cable_diams: [4.0, 6.0, 8.0] or length: 300.0, rerun the script, and out comes a new mesh sized for your drawer, your cable, or your seed packets.

The scripts are plain Python built on trimesh, and each one also accepts parameter overrides as a JSON argument on the command line, so you can rebuild without touching the file. Run it once unchanged to reproduce our exact STL, run it again with your own values for a variant. Each script prints a mesh check as it finishes: watertight, manifold, bounding box, volume, so you can confirm the rebuilt part is sound before you slice it.

Everything in the table below exists as renders and mesh-checked geometry; none of the ten parts has been test printed yet. The meshes passed watertight and manifold checks before upload, and the MakerWorld descriptions say the same.

How a rebuild works

Take the hex key gauge. Its generator defines the size list af_sizes: [1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10] and a 0.3 mm clearance on every cutout, so a nominal 5 mm key seats in a 5.3 mm hole. If you keep an odd set of keys, you pass your own list, rerun, and the gauge regrows around it: plate length, hole count, and the engraved labels all follow the parameter. The same pattern runs through the whole set. The seed ruler regrows its V notches at your pitches. The bit holder lays out whatever grid you give it. The bag clip deepens its throat to your bag fold.

Wall widths in the shipped files target a standard 0.4 mm nozzle. The cable clips run 2.4 mm walls, which is three perimeters, so a rebuilt clip prints as cleanly as the original as long as you keep walls on that multiple or the slicer's own perimeter width. Every part sits flat on the plate with no supports; the generators cut openings upward or straight through for that reason.

Where to get the files

The cable clip set is live on MakerWorld; the other nine were in MakerWorld's review queue when this page was published and their pages appear as review clears. Everything lands on the @jayjey profile, each download includes its gen_*.py generator, and all ten carry CC BY 4.0, so remixing and republishing is allowed with attribution.
