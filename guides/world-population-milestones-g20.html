World population milestones through G20 data, 1960 to 2025

World Bank WDI series SP.POP.TOTL, retrieved 2026-09-10 (WDI update 2026-07-13), published 2026-09-10. Data source: World Bank Open Data, CC BY 4.0. Data Vault is not affiliated with the World Bank. This page documents the data; it is not demographic or policy advice.

What this page measures

Every number here comes from the free CSV behind World Bank Population, G20 Countries + EU27: 1,320 rows covering the 19 national G20 members plus the EU27 aggregate, one row per entity-year from 1960 through 2025. The values are the World Bank's own, kept verbatim; nothing is rounded or imputed. When a milestone below says a country crossed a line in a given year, that is the first year in the series where the number sits above the line.

One convention runs through the whole page: the G20 seats the European Union alongside the 19 countries, and the EU27 aggregate (451,127,411 in 2025, marked EUU in the ISO3 column) is not a country. Sums and club counts on this page cover the 19 national members only.

The crossover: India passes China

The most-watched number in world demography shows up directly in the rows. China led India by 231 million people in 1960 (667,070,000 against 435,990,338). The gap narrowed every decade, and in 2021 the series flips: India 1,414,203,896, China 1,412,360,000. The first margin was thin at 1,843,896 people, roughly a mid-sized city, and it widens every year after.

Milestone years in the series

The 66-year file catches most of the world's headline population crossings as they happened, inside these 19 countries alone:

  • China passes 1 billion in 1982 (1,008,630,000), the first member past the line. India follows in 1998.
  • The 100-million club doubles. Four members were over 100 million in 1960 (China, India, the United States, Russia). Indonesia joined by 1965, Japan in 1968 (101,011,000), Brazil in 1972, and Mexico in 2001 (100,099,099). Eight of the 19 were over 100 million in 2025.
  • The United States passes 300 million in 2007 (301,231,207), up from 180,671,000 in 1960.
  • China passes 1.4 billion in 2018; India follows two years later, in 2020, on its way past China.
  • Brazil passes 200 million in 2014 (212,812,405 in 2025).

India and China remain in a tier of their own: 2,870,450,525 people between them, 60.5 percent of the 19-country total. The United States is a distant third at 341,784,857.

Six of nineteen are already past peak

A population peak is the highest value an entity reaches in the series. Thirteen of the 19 national members set their record in 2025. The other six peaked earlier, and their lines have turned down:

Russia's decline is the longest, 33 years and counting. Germany's is the newest and the smallest, 25,344 people in its first year below the 2024 record, so one more year of data will say whether it is a trend or a wobble. China's is the largest in absolute terms. Whether the other 13 members hold their 2025 records is exactly what the yearly rows in the CSV will show.

Where the growth went

Growth since 1960 is wildly uneven across the set. Saudi Arabia is the outlier: 2,435,403 people in 1960 became 36,973,555 in 2025, a 15.18× increase, by far the fastest in the G20. South Africa follows at 3.94×, then Mexico 3.59×, India 3.36×, Indonesia 3.24×, and Türkiye 3.03×. Australia grew 2.69× and the United States 1.89×.

The slowest growers are the countries from the peak table: Russia at 1.20×, Italy at 1.17×, and Germany at 1.15× over the same 65 years. The 15.18× to 1.15× spread is the widest in the set, and both countries sit in the same table of the same CSV.

Read the series yourself

The full 1,320-row file downloads free on the dataset page under CC BY 4.0, alongside a 20-row latest-year file (g20-population-2025.csv) and a schema. Four columns: entity, iso3, year, population. Load it in pandas or a spreadsheet, filter out EUU when you want national sums, and every table above reproduces in minutes. If you republish derived numbers, the dataset license guide covers what CC BY 4.0 attribution asks of you.

For the labour side of the same economies, the Eurostat unemployment guide works through a companion CSV with harmonised unemployment rates for the EU-27 and eight more countries, 2003-2025, and the Eurostat unemployment dataset pairs directly with this population file for per-capita style analysis.

The rural half of the same rows is its own guide: the rural population share by country guide follows the same 20 entities from eight majority-rural members in 1960 down to India alone in 2025, at 64.31 percent and about 941 million people. The health side of the same entities is the life expectancy by country guide: Japan's 53-year run at the top, China's 44.60-year climb off the file's lowest value, and the 2021 COVID troughs entity by entity.

World population milestone questions

When did India pass China in population?
India passed China in 2021, the first year the World Bank series shows India ahead: 1,414,203,896 against 1,412,360,000, a margin of 1,843,896. By 2025 the lead had grown to 57,280,525, with India at 1,463,865,525 and China down to 1,406,585,000.
Which G20 countries have passed their population peak?
Six of the 19 national members in the 2025 data sit below their all-time peak: Russia (peaked 1992 at 148,538,197), Japan (2010, 128,070,000), Italy (2014, 60,320,707), South Korea (2020, 51,836,239), China (2021, 1,412,360,000), and Germany (2024, 83,516,593). The other 13 members were still at record populations in 2025.
How many people live in the G20?
The 19 national members of the G20 count 4,744,611,663 people in the 2025 World Bank figures, about 58 percent of the world. India and China together account for 2,870,450,525 of that, 60.5 percent of the national total. The G20 also includes the European Union as a member; its EU27 aggregate of 451,127,411 is a separate row in the data and is excluded from the 19-country sum.
