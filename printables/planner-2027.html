2027 dated planner and wall calendar: 92 pages, 53 weekly spreads, one PDF

92 pages53 weekly spreadsA4 + Letter15 mm marginsblack and white

A full year of 2027 in one PDF you print yourself: dated weeklies on Monday-first spreads, twelve full-page wall months, monthly plan sheets, and refill cards cut to drop back into the binder. Both the A4 and US Letter files carry the same 92 pages, so paper size is the only choice you make.

The dated run starts Monday 28 December 2026 and ends Sunday 2 January 2028. That is deliberate: 2027 opens on a Friday, so a planner that started its first spread on 1 January would leave three days of the year with no page. 53 spreads x 7 columns = 371 dated cells: all 365 days of 2027, plus the four leftover days of 2026 and the first two of 2028.

How it prints

  • Print at 100% scale, or "actual size". Fit-to-page shrinks the writing areas and the habit grid starts losing cells.
  • Duplex, flip on the long edge, for the weekly spreads and monthly plans. Print the wall months one-sided so pins and tape do not cross writing.
  • Plain 80 gsm paper is fine. Use 100 gsm if you write hard or use a fountain pen, since the pages are single-sided by design for the wall blocks.
  • Everything is black and white and stays readable in toner-save mode.
  • Refill cards: cut on the dashed line, punch on the three circles, and the card drops into a ring or disc-bound planner in place of the sheet you used up.

Personal use. Print as many copies as your household needs; do not resell or republish the pages.

How the files were checked

Both PDFs are 92 pages and the page-tree graph passes: 92 of 92 /Kids nodes reachable, 92 of 92 /Contents streams present, no orphan pages, /Count 92 against an expected 92. A build-level QA script re-derives all 53 ISO week numbers and date ranges against Python's calendar, checks the 371 weekly day cells, checks each month grid length against the real month, and checks the page footer on all 92 pages.

Rebuilding the generator twice produced the same checksum for the A4 file (md5 69b1a7adce878f5fecacc6f89440ebfb, Letter 95ab458e86a3b41cd9a0ded7b17d683e). Layout was verified from per-page text coordinates rather than eyeballed screenshots, so no page image has been proofed on paper here: if the margins read tight on your printer, they are tight on every page.
